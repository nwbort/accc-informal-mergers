Background 
The parties to the Proposed Transaction 
2.1 
Acquirer: A consortium comprising Brookfield, its affiliates and its co-investors and Temasek 
proposes, via Brookfield Renewable Holdings SAS (a wholly owned subsidiary of Bernabeu 
Master UK Holdings Limited), to acquire up to 100% of the issued capital in Neoen SA.  
2.2 
Brookfield Infrastructure (through Brookfield Super-Core Infrastructure Partners) has a 45.4% 
interest in AusNet and the balance is held by a number of unrelated parties. Brookfield actively 
manages AusNet. AusNet is an Australian energy delivery services group that owns and operates 
the main monopoly Victorian transmission network, and one of the five electricity distributors in 
Victoria. AusNet has two battery energy storage systems and a further two development projects 
in Victoria.  
2.3 
Target: Neoen SA (Neoen) specialises in the development, construction, financing, operation 
and management of renewable energy projects. In Australia, Neoen has a portfolio of utility-scale 
renewable energy projects across several States and Territories. 
The Proposed Transaction 
2.4 
On 29 May 2024, Brookfield entered into exclusive negotiations to acquire 53.32% of the issued 
shares in Neoen SA from several independent sellers (the Block Acquisition). Following 
completion of the Block Acquisition, Brookfield proposes to launch a mandatory simplified tender 
offer for remaining securities issued by Neoen (together with the Block Acquisition, referred to as 
the Proposed Transaction). Following completion of the Proposed Transaction, the Brookfield 
consortium would have up to 100% of the issued shares in Neoen SA.  
The ACCC's Review 
2.5 
On 12 July 2024 the ACCC commenced its public review of the Proposed Transaction. 
2.6 
Brookfield offered to provide an undertaking pursuant to which it would divest Neoen's operating 
assets and development projects in Victoria (the Divestiture Business, as defined in Schedule 4 
and Confidential Schedule 6). 
2.7 
The ACCC undertook market inquiries and considered information provided by the parties to the 
to assess whether the Proposed Transaction would have the effect, or be likely to have the effect, 
of substantially lessening competition in any market in Australia in contravention of section 50 of 
the Act. 
The ACCC's competition concerns 
2.8

would have the effect, or be likely to have the effect of substantially lessening competition in the 
markets for: 
(a) 
the supply of new renewable generation, the supply of new firming capacity and/or the 
supply of new electricity storage services in Victoria in response to government policy

waiver must be express and in writing.
