The ACCC's review
On 4 February 2015, the ACCC commenced its public review of the Proposed Transaction.
2.13 The ACCC undertook marketinquiries and considered information provided by the parties to the
Proposed Transaction. industry participants and others. The purpose of the ACCC's inquiries was
to assess whether the Proposed Transaction would have the effect. or be likely to have the effect,
of substantially lessening competition in any market in contravention of section 50 of the Act.
2.12
The ACCC's competition concerns
2.14 The ACCC has concluded that, in the absence of the Undertaking, the Proposed Transaction
would have the effect, or be likely to have the effect, of substantially lessening competition in
relation to the supply of shopping centre amenities to customers (shoppers) in South East
Melbourne. The ACCC also had competition concerns aboutthe effect of the proposed
transaction in relation to the supply of retail space in shopping centres to retailers in South East
Melbourne, as set out below
2.15 The AGCC considered that a starting pointfor assessing the impact of the Proposed Transaction
in particular geographic areas was to assess the level of competition provided by alternative
shopping centres within approximately 10 kilometres of any relevant overlap between shopping
centres owned by Federation Centres and Novion
On this basis, the ACCC's review focused on the South East Melbourne geographic region,
where it considered that the Bayside shopping centre (owned by Novion) and the Karingal Hub
shopping centre (part owned by Federation Centres), were the only multi-purpose shopping
centres of any significant scale within approximately 20km of the Frankston CBD. The ACCC
considered that Bayside and Kanngal Hub were each other's closest competitors in the supply of
shopping centre amenities to customers, and in supplying retail space in shopping centres to
2.16
page 2


retailers. in this geographic region, and that the aggregation of these centres could result in anti-
competitive effects.
The supply of shopping centre amenities to customersin South EastMelboume
2.17 The ACCC was concerned that common ownership of Bayside and Kanngal Hub would have the
potential to reduce the incentive of the merged entity to expand and develop Bayside and/or
Karingal Hub. This was because a portion of the expected gains from any such development for
each individual centre. under separate ownership, would have come from attracting customers
which would otherwise visitthe other centre; under common ownership this would not be
regarded as an overall gain to the merged entity. This reduced incentive to expand and develop
the two centres had the potential to result in reduced amenities and retail offerings being supplied
to shoppers in the region when compared to the position in the absence of the Proposed
Transaction
2.18 The ACCC also considered that the merged entity would have had the ability and incentive to
operate Bayside and Kanngal Hub in a way which maximised total rental yields across the two
centres. The ACCC considered that this may have involved selecting a mix oftenants which less
directly overlap and compete between the two centres, which could have represented a loss of
convenience and potentially price competition for shoppers in the local area.
The supply of retailspace in shopping centres to retailers in South EastMelboume
2.19 The ACCC was also concerned aboutthe possible future ability of the merged entity to extract
higher rents from retailers seeking to lease space in South East Melbourne, particularly retailers
relying on comparison shopping such as clothing, footwear, jewellery and gift stores. This is
because of the lack of other regional and sub-regional shopping centres in the local area, and the
very limited ability of retailers to substitute to other forms of retail space in the local area
The Undertaking remedy
Federation Centres and Federation Manager do not consider that the Proposed Transaction
2.20
would be likely to substantially lessen competition in the relevant markets. However, as part of
Federation Centres' and Novion's request for informal clearance of the Proposed Transaction.
and in order to address the ACCC's competition concerns. Federation Centres and Federation
Manager have, without admission, offered this Undertaking under section 87B of the Act to divest
the Kanngal Hub Divestiture Property to a purchaser approved by the ACCC, unless notice has

form requiring that it may divest the Bayside Divestiture Property, unless notice has been
provided pursuant to this Undertaking that the Karingal Hub Divestiture Property will be divested.

The ACCC's competition concerns
The ACCC has concluded that, in the absence of the Undertaking, the Proposed Transact'
2.14
would have the effect, or be likely to have the effect, of substantially lessening coin etition in
relation to the supply of shopping centre amenities to customers (shoppers) in South East
Melbourne. The ACCC also had competition concerns aboutthe effect of the proposed
transaction in relation to the supply of retail space in shopping centres to retailers in South East
Melbourne, as set out below.
The ACCC considered that a starting pointfor assessing the impact of the Pro OSed Tran t'
in particular geographic areas was to assess the level of competition provided b alternative
shopping centres within approximately 10 kilometres of any relevant overlap between sho in
centres owned by Federation Centres and Novion
On this basis, the AGCC's reviewfocused on the South East Melbourne eo re hic re ' ,
where it considered that the Bayside shopping centre (owned by Novion) and the Karingal Hub

shopping centre (part owned by Federation Centres), were the only multi-purpose shopping

The supply of shopping centre amenities to customers in South East Melbourne
2.17
The ACCC was concerned that common ownership of Bayside and Kanngal Hub would have th
potential to reduce the incentive of the merged entity to expand and develop Bayside and/or
Karingal Hub. This was because a portion of the expected gains from any such develo meritfor
each individual centre, under separate ownership, would have come from attracting customers
which would otherwise visitthe other centre; under common ownership this would not be
regarded as an overall gain to the merged entity. This reduced incentive to expand and develo
the two centres had the potential to result in reduced amenities and retail offerin s bein su lied
to shoppers in the region when compared to the position in the absence of the Proposed
Transaction.
The ACCC also considered that the merged entity would have had the ability and incentive to
operate Bayside and Karingal Hub in a way which maximised total rental yields across the two
centres. The ACCC considered that this may have involved selecting a mix oftenants which less
directly overlap and compete between the two centres, which could have represented a loss of
convenience and potentially price competition for shoppers in the local area.
The supply of retailspace in shopping centres to retailers in South East Melbourne
The ACCC was also concerned aboutthe possible future ability of the merged entit to extract
2.19
higher rents from retailers, seeking to lease space in South East Melbourne, particularly retailers
relying on comparison shopping such as clothing, footwear, jewellery and gift stores. This is
because of the lack of other regional and sub-regional shopping centres in the local area, and
the very limited ability of retailers to substitute to other forms of retail space in the local area.
