Dear Interested Party 
Request for submissions: BP - proposed acquisition of Woolworths' retail 
service station sites (local market issues) 
The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) is seeking your views on 
the proposed acquisition of Woolworths Limited’s (Woolworths) network of retail service 
station sites by BP Australia Pty Ltd (BP) (the proposed acquisition). 
Further details regarding the proposed acquisition can be found in the Statement of Issues 
published by the ACCC on 10 August 2017, available here. 
As explained in the Statement of Issues, the ACCC has expressed preliminary concerns 
that the proposed acquisition may substantially lessen competition in local retail fuel 
markets surrounding some of the Woolworths sites that BP proposes to acquire.  
These local market issues sit alongside competition issues related to broader metropolitan-
wide retail fuel markets, which the ACCC is continuing to investigate.  
The purpose of this supplementary market inquiries letter is to seek comments on a 
preliminary screen of local markets.  We are seeking comments from industry participants 
on specific local markets where the overlap between BP and Woolworths may raise local 
competition concerns.  
BP proposes to acquire 531 existing sites from Woolworths, and 14 sites under 
development. The preliminary screening aims to identify which of those sites require further 
analysis. The screening criteria are designed to that end and should not be interpreted as 
criteria for determining whether a substantial lessening of competition is, in fact, likely to 
arise in a particular local market. 
It is also important to recognise, as noted above, that the ACCC is also separately 
considering competition issues in broader metropolitan-wide markets. 
Discussion of the ACCC’s local market screening methodology and results are set out in 
Attachment A. 
This matter is public and you can forward this letter to anybody who may be interested. 
The legal test which the ACCC applies in considering the proposed acquisition is in section 
50 of the Competition and Consumer Act 2010. Section 50 prohibits acquisitions that are 
likely to have the effect of substantially lessening competition in a market.

Attachment A 
The table in this attachment sets out a preliminary screening of local markets where the 
ACCC seeks further comment from industry participants. 
This initial screening does not reflect detailed analysis of critical issues that are relevant to 
local market competition issues, such as: 
 
the closeness of competition between the BP and Woolworths sites; 
 
traffic flows; 
 
topography; 
 
the volumes sold at each site; and 
 
the degree to which the other sites compete with BP and/or Woolworths.  
A more detailed analysis will be undertaken on sites identified by the initial screening as 
requiring further assessment, taking into consideration feedback the ACCC receives from 
industry participants.   
In constructing this preliminary list of local markets the ACCC has taken considerable care to 
ensure its accuracy. This includes information requests to retailers, checks of existing site 
lists and data, and the use of the ACCC’s compulsory information gathering powers. 
Nonetheless, given the large number of sites involved the list is subject to a number of 
assumptions and is likely to contain some inaccuracies. The ACCC also understands that in 
some instances sites may have opened, closed or changed hands since the ACCC obtained 
information about them.  
The ACCC also wishes to emphasise that the list is not a list of local markets where the 
ACCC will have competition concerns.  The list only reflects an initial and approximate 
screening methodology.  
Treatment of BP Resellers 
BP supplies fuel to Australian consumers both directly through retail sites owned and 
operated by BP (BP COCO) and indirectly through a network of sites owned and operated 
by third-party resellers (BP Resellers), who set their own retail fuel prices.  
Because BP operates both a retail and wholesale business, the nature of the competition 
between BP COCO and BP Reseller sites may be different to competition between 
competitors where this relationship does not exist.  
The ACCC is yet to reach a concluded view on how BP Resellers should be treated for the 
purpose of its competition analysis. The ACCC has therefore considered two different 
approaches for the purpose of this screening: 
 
The column labelled “Primary screen: by site operator” shows the results of a 
screening which considers BP Resellers as separate competitors to BP COCO 
sites. 
 
The column labelled “Secondary screen: BP branded sites” shows the results 
of the screening analysis where both BP COCO sites and BP Resellers are 
aggregated for the purpose of calculating market shares.   
The list attached does not include sites where a current Woolworths site may be within the 
local area of a BP Reseller, but not a BP COCO site. However, the ACCC welcomes 
submissions on such local areas where industry participants may have concerns.

Criteria 
Market radius 
The ACCC has considered different areas around each Woolworths site depending on 
whether that Woolworths site is located in a metropolitan or regional area. This is because 
the area of close competition between sites is likely to be larger in regional areas than in 
metropolitan areas.1 
 
For each metropolitan site we have considered a 3km radius around the site 
(metropolitan includes major regional cities/towns).  
 
For each regional site we have considered a radius of 10km around the site.  
The ACCC has taken this approach for the purposes of this market screening, and does not 
consider that the boundaries of local markets will be the same in each metropolitan market, 
and in each regional market. Every market has different competitive dynamics, and 
accordingly the market boundaries will differ in every case.  
Market concentration 
Because the purpose of this document is to set out a preliminary screening only, the ACCC 
has considered local areas where the BP and Woolworths’ combined share of sites would 
exceed 25%, or where there would be less than three remaining third-party retailers (i.e. not 
counting BP) in the local area post-acquisition.  These thresholds should not be interpreted 
as levels above which competition concerns are likely to arise. There are likely to be many 
sites in the list where no competition concerns are likely to arise. 
Further, there may be sites that do not reach these thresholds where there could still be 
competition concerns (for example, if a BP site and Woolworths site in a regional area were 
immediate neighbours, and there were many competing sites 9 kms away, this is a situation 
where there may be competition concerns, but the initial screening thresholds are not 
breached).  
When calculating the number of remaining competitors within the local markets post-
acquisition, the ACCC treats multiple sites owned by a single player as a single competitor.  
However, when calculating the market share columns, each individual site is given equal 
weighting.  The ACCC has often had to treat some independent sites as if they are 
controlled by a single entity, as information regarding ownership of independent sites is 
limited in some circumstances.  When a more detailed analysis is undertaken on each local 
market, the ACCC will investigate ownership of the local sites in the relevant local markets 
more carefully where relevant.  
The ACCC’s view on each local market will evolve as further analysis is undertaken.
