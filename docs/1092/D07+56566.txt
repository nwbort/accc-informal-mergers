 
 
 
Public Competition Assessment 
26 June 2007 
Woolworths Limited - proposed acquisition of Jindabyne IGA 
Supermarket, Festival IGA Liquor, and Porter's Liquor licence 
Introduction 
1. 
On 19 June 2007, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission 
(ACCC) announced its decision not to oppose the proposed acquisition of 
Jindabyne Festival IGA Supermarket, Jindabyne Festival IGA Liquor and the 
licence only of Porter's Liquor by Woolworths Limited (proposed acquisition).  
The ACCC was of the view that the proposed acquisition would be unlikely to 
have the effect of substantially lessening competition in the relevant markets in 
contravention of section 50 of the Trade Practices Act 1974 (the Act). 
2. 
The ACCC formed its view on the basis of the information provided by the 
merger parties and information arising from its market inquiries.  This Public 
Competition Assessment outlines the basis on which the ACCC has reached its 
decision on the proposed acquisition, subject to confidentiality considerations. 
Public Competition Assessment 
3. 
To provide an enhanced level of transparency and procedural fairness in its 
decision making process, the ACCC issues a Public Competition Assessment for 
all transaction proposals where: 
 a merger is rejected; 
 a merger is subject to enforceable undertakings; 
 the merger parties seek such disclosure; or 
 a merger is approved but raises important issues that the ACCC considers 
should be made public. 
4. 
This Public Competition Assessment has been issued because the proposed 
acquisition is considered to raise issues of interest to the public. 
5. 
By issuing Public Competition Assessments, the ACCC aims to provide the 
market with a better understanding of the ACCC's analysis of various markets 
and the associated merger and competition issues.  It also alerts the market to the 
circumstances where the ACCC’s assessment of the competition conditions in 
particular markets is changing, or likely to change, because of developments.   
6. 
Each Public Competition Assessment is specific to the particular transaction 
under review by the ACCC.  While some transaction proposals may involve the

The parties 
The acquirer: Woolworths Limited 
8. 
Woolworths Limited (Woolworths) is Australia’s largest grocery retailer.  It 
operates 761 supermarkets across Australia and is the owner, or joint owner, of 
1018 retail liquor outlets.  Woolworths owns two supermarkets and associated 
liquor stores in Cooma. 
The target: Jindabyne IGA Supermarket, Jindabyne Festival IGA Liquor and the 
licence only of Porter's Liquor 
9. 
The assets to be acquired, namely Jindabyne Festival IGA Supermarket 
(Jindabyne IGA), Jindabyne Festival IGA Liquor (Jindabyne Festival Liquor) 
and the licence only of Porter's Liquor (Porter’s) are owned by Aymblack Pty 
Ltd.  Aymblack is owned by Mr Bruce Marshall.  Mr Marshall is also the 
ultimate owner of Jindabyne IGA Express Supermarket (IGA Express), Thredbo 
IGA Supermarket, Perisher IGA Supermarket and a small convenience store at 
Lake Crackenback resort, none of which will be acquired by Woolworths.  All of 
Mr Marshall’s stores are supplied by Metcash.  Mr Marshall’s liquor stores are 
supplied by ALM, Metcash’s liquor arm. 
Other industry participants 
10. 
Coles Group Ltd will open a supermarket in Cooma in April or May 2008.  There 
are also a number of independent convenience stores in the towns surrounding 
Jindabyne at Berridale, Bombala, Nimmitabel, Delegate and Adaminaby.   
Background 
11. 
Jindabyne is a small town in a rural area of NSW, with a resident population of 
around 4 320 (14 000 in June –September).  It has one supermarket and one 
convenience store.  Jindabyne is the gateway to the Snowy Mountains and is 
therefore heavily dependent on income from tourists, as is the supermarket.

12. 
Cooma is 62km from Jindabyne and is the largest town in the region, with a 
resident population of around 8 000.  Cooma acts as a commercial centre for 
around 31 000 residents in the Cooma-Monaro region.  There are two 
Woolworths supermarkets in Cooma and a Coles supermarket is under 
construction, scheduled to open in April or May 2008.  There are also small 
convenience stores at Berridale (30km from Jindabyne), Thredbo Village (35km 
from Jindabyne), Lake Crackenback Resort (19 km from Jindabyne), Perisher 
Village (33km from Jindabyne), Bombala (107 km from Jindabyne), Nimmitabel 
(99 km from Jindabyne), Delegate (144 km from Jindabyne), and Adaminaby (85 
km from Jindabyne).   
13. 
Jindabyne IGA Supermarket and Jindabyne Festival Liquor are situated in the 
same premises within the Nuggets Crossing shopping centre in Jindabyne.  The 
Jindabyne IGA is a full size, or near full size, supermarket, with 11 checkouts, 
although it only operates at capacity in the winter peak.  Liquor sales account for 
a substantial proportion of the turnover of the Jindabyne IGA.   
14. 
The Porter’s Liquor outlet is situated within the same premises as the Jindabyne 
IGA Express, which is located about 80m from the IGA Jindabyne.  The IGA 
Express is operated as a convenience store.  It is around 1/3 the size of the 
Jindabyne IGA Supermarket and stays open an hour or two longer depending 
upon season.  Liquor sales are also a substantial proportion of the turnover in the 
Express Supermarket.   
The proposed transaction 
15. 
Woolworths proposes to acquire Jindabyne IGA Supermarket, Jindabyne Festival 
IGA Liquor and the Porter's Liquor licence from Aymblack.   
Areas of Overlap 
16. 
Although they are geographically separate, Woolworths and the assets it proposes 
to acquire overlap in the product markets of supermarket and liquor retailing.

Market inquiries 
18. 
The ACCC conducted market inquiries with a range of industry participants, 
including competitors, potential competitors, customers, input suppliers, industry 
bodies and other interested parties.  Submissions were sought via a market 
inquiries letter and telephone inquiries in relation to the substantive competition 
issues. 
With/without test 
19. 
In assessing a merger pursuant to section 50 of the Act, the ACCC must consider 
the effects of the transaction by comparing the likely competitive environment 
post-merger if the transaction proceeds (the “with” position) to the likely 
competitive environment post-merger if the transaction does not proceed (the 
“without” position).  This is a forward looking exercise.  Consequently, if the 
most likely scenario in the absence of the merger is for IGA Jindabyne and IGA 
Express to be sold to (or otherwise held by) separate owners, then the ACCC’s 
consideration should take into account this potential for future competition in the 
absence of the merger, regardless of the fact that the 2 outlets are currently held 
by one owner.   
20. 
Mr Marshall has received bids from a number of other interested parties.  The 
ACCC found that if the Woolworths acquisition did not proceed, then it was 
highly likely that Mr Marshall would sell one or both of the Jindabyne stores to 
one of the other interested parties.   
Market One – Supermarket Retailing 
Market definition 
21. 
In defining relevant markets, the ACCC takes a purposive approach.  The ACCC 
formed the view that one relevant market was the Jindabyne/Cooma market for 
supermarket retailing.  This geographic market is broader than markets 
considered in some previous supermarket acquisitions, due to factors unique to 
this transaction. 
22. 
Consistent with previous supermarket analyses, the ACCC took into account 
local factors in defining the relevant market.  These include the fact that most 
customers of the Jindabyne supermarkets travel through Cooma on their way to 
Jindabyne (if they are tourists) or travel from Jindabyne to or through Cooma on 
a regular basis for employment, to access government services, to attend weekend 
sporting events and for other reasons (if they are residents).   
23. 
A substantial proportion of those who live in or visit Jindabyne either do grocery 
shopping in Cooma, or could do so if they wished to, without the need to travel to 
Cooma specifically for the purpose of grocery shopping.  The Cooma 
supermarkets are also each around 30% larger than the Jindabyne IGA and are 
considered to have lower prices on many items.  Accordingly, market inquiries 
indicate that they have a larger drawing area than does the Jindabyne 
supermarket.

24. 
These factors indicate that the supermarkets at Cooma are far more likely to 
impose some constraint on the IGA Jindabyne supermarket than would be the 
case for two supermarkets located 60km apart in a metropolitan area.  
Accordingly, the local markets for supermarket retailing are significantly larger 
than the 3-5km radius normally considered by the ACCC in metropolitan 
supermarket acquisitions.   
25. 
While the Cooma supermarkets impose a constraint on the Jindabyne 
supermarkets, this appears to be asymmetric; that is, the Jindabyne supermarkets 
impose only a very weak constraint, if any, on the Cooma supermarkets.  Most 
Cooma residents do not need to travel to Jindabyne on a regular basis, and would 
therefore have to make a dedicated trip to the smaller IGA Jindabyne supermarket 
if they wished to switch away from their local Woolworths supermarket.   
26. 
Evidence indicates that the Cooma stores and the Jindabyne stores advertise in 
both towns, although for some advertising media this may be because the 
circulation or broadcast areas spill over to non-target audiences.   
Conclusion on Market Definition in Market 1 
27. 
The evidence indicates that, as the nearest large supermarkets, the Cooma 
supermarkets exert some competitive constraint upon the Jindabyne 
supermarkets.  The IGA Express in Jindabyne, which is currently owned by the 
owner of the IGA Jindabyne supermarket, is likely to impose a degree of 
constraint on the Woolworths supermarket post-acquisition.  However, this is 
more likely to be in relation to convenience and service levels than price, given 
that the Express store is considered to have higher prices.  Customers are more 
likely to switch to IGA Express on the basis that it has shorter queues, better 
parking and faster service at peak times, than they are to switch in response to a 
price rise by Woolworths.   
28. 
It appears unlikely that the smaller convenience store-type supermarkets in the 
towns around Jindabyne exert any constraint upon the supermarkets in Jindabyne.  
For the reasons outlined above, it appears unlikely that the Jindabyne IGA 
supermarket strongly constrains the Cooma supermarkets (in other words, the 
competitive constraint is binding in only one direction).  The acquisition has 
therefore been examined within the geographic market of Jindabyne and Cooma. 
29. 
If a narrower market is considered, there can be no lessening of competition, 
since the acquirer (Woolworths) would not be otherwise active in the relevant 
market. 
Competition analysis 
Market concentration 
30. 
Post acquisition, Woolworths will own a supermarket in Cooma, a supermarket in 
Cooma South and the larger of the two Jindabyne grocery retailers.  Mr Marshall 
will retain the convenience-type Express Supermarket at Jindabyne but may 
choose to sell it to another buyer later.  Coles is currently constructing a 
supermarket in Cooma, which is expected to open in April or May 2008.

31. 
Post acquisition, the former Jindabyne IGA (now Woolworths) supermarket will 
no longer be constrained by the threat of losing customers to Woolworths in 
Cooma.  The ACCC examined the theory that Woolworths would have an 
incentive to increase prices at Jindabyne in the belief that any customers lost 
would go instead to Woolworths Cooma (i.e. they would not be lost to 
Woolworths).   
32. 
However, it appears likely that once it opens, Coles in Cooma will provide a 
constraint on the Jindabyne store similar to the constraint that Woolworths 
Cooma currently imposes on IGA Jindabyne.  Further, this Coles store will also 
constrain the Cooma stores.  Given its proximity, size, and likely prices, the 
Coles supermarket is likely to be far better able to constrain Woolworths at 
Cooma than the IGA Jindabyne currently is. 
33. 
The IGA Express convenience store that will remain in Jindabyne may also 
provide some constraint on the pricing, or more likely the service levels, of 
Woolworths at Jindabyne.  No such constraint currently exists between the two 
Jindabyne retailers, since both are owned by Aymblack.  However, IGA 
Express’s ability to impose a strong constraint will be limited by its size and the 
lack of an associated liquor licence.   
Substitutes 
34. 
Tourists may choose to bring some or all of their grocery supplies with them to 
the area.  Tourists account for a substantial proportion of the sales at the 
Jindabyne IGA.  Therefore, this practice is likely to be a partial substitute for the 
Jindabyne IGA, and it is conceivable that some price sensitive tourists would 
bring their own groceries and alcohol on their next trip to the area if prices rose 
significantly at the Jindabyne outlet.   
Barriers to entry 
A.  Access to scarce retail sites 
35. 
This may be a barrier to entry, particularly for a new entrant to the national 
grocery industry or for a small independent, since a landlord may prefer an 
established supermarket chain as a tenant.  Accessing sites is not a significant 
barrier to expansion for large supermarket chains wishing to enter new local 
markets, provided that sites exist.  It is unclear whether there are any further sites 
in Jindabyne suitable for supermarkets, with market participants providing 
differing submissions to the ACCC on this subject. 
B.  Potential for Expansion of the Jindabyne Express Supermarket 
36. 
There is very limited ability for the Jindabyne Express Supermarket to physically 
expand.  It is located at the end of a strip of shops.  Beyond the shopping strip is 
the public toilets and town hall.  A café with a floor area of around 100 m2 is on 
the other side of the supermarket.

37. 
This may be a barrier to smaller scale entry as some of the costs associated with 
operating a retail grocery store may be shared between stores, leading to lower 
costs per store for large chains.  These include costs for advertising, store fit-out 
and rental costs.  These economies would not be a barrier for Coles and 
Woolworths given their size.   
38. 
In regional centres there appears to be some potential to use scale in order to save 
transport costs.  For the present, Mr Marshall will continue to own all of the 
affected stores, and will therefore bear any transport related cost increases as a 
result of his reduced grocery volumes, should they occur.  It appears unlikely that 
the increase in costs will be significant.   
Grocery/liquor bundling 
39. 
IGA Express/Porter’s Liquor is the closest potential competitor in Jindabyne to 
Jindabyne IGA/Festival Liquor, as it offers a similar food/liquor bundle.  The loss 
of the Porter’s liquor licence is likely to weaken the IGA Express store.  
However, despite the weakening of the IGA Express Store, the acquisition as a 
whole may still lead to more competition within Jindabyne as the ownership of 
the IGA Express will be separated from the ownership of the Jindabyne IGA, as 
opposed to the present situation where both are owned by Aymblack.   
Conclusion on market one 
40. 
It appears unlikely that the acquisition will lead to a substantial lessening of 
competition in the local supermarket market surrounding the Jindabyne IGA 
Supermarket.  Although the Woolworths stores in Cooma will no longer provide 
any constraint on the Jindabyne supermarket, the opening of Coles in April/May 
2008 is likely to provide an equivalent constraint.   
Market Two – Liquor retailing market 
Market definition 
41. 
The ACCC considered that another of the relevant markets in this matter was the 
Jindabyne market in liquor retailing for off-premises consumption.   
42. 
The larger number of liquor outlets than supermarkets in Jindabyne and past 
analysis of liquor matters tends to indicate that convenience is a more important 
factor in liquor retail than in supermarket retail, therefore competition may take 
place at a more local level.  However, a significant number of the people who do 
grocery shopping when they travel to or through Cooma purchase alcohol at the 
same time.   
43. 
Given the available information and the fact that many of the same considerations 
relevant to the above analysis of the grocery market are also likely to apply to the 
liquor market, the ACCC found that Jindabyne liquor retailers are likely to be 
constrained to some degree by the Cooma liquor retailers.  Therefore, the ACCC 
considered the acquisition on the basis of a liquor retailing market that includes 
Cooma.

Competition analysis 
Market concentration 
45. 
After the proposed acquisition Woolworths will own the largest liquor retailing 
businesses in Jindabyne (Jindabyne Festival Liquor).  IGA Express/Porter’s 
Liquor is the closest liquor retailer to Jindabyne IGA/Jindabyne Festival Liquor 
and has the second largest floor area devoted to liquor after Jindabyne Festival 
Liquor.   
46. 
Pre acquisition, Aymblack owns both of these liquor licences, so they do not 
compete with each other.  Post-acquisition, Woolworths will own both licences, 
so the concentration of ownership of licences in Jindabyne will be unchanged, 
although only licence will be traded. 
47. 
As it does not intend to trade the Porter’s Liquor licence from its present location, 
the acquisition will mean that Woolworths will also own the only store that can 
offer a food/liquor bundle (Jindabyne IGA/Jindabyne Festival Liquor).  The 
ACCC notes that pre-merger, there is also only one owner of stores offering both 
groceries and liquor in Jindabyne, namely Aymblack. It is unclear whether, in the 
absence of the proposed acquisition, IGA Express would be sold together with 
the Porter’s Liquor licence to a separate buyer. If it were, this would create a 
second player offering groceries and liquor, although the IGA Express operates as 
a small format convenience store, which would be unlikely to impose a strong 
pricing constraint on a full-range supermarket. 
48. 
Due to the relatively larger size of the Festival IGA Liquor store being acquired 
compared with its remaining competitors, in addition to the advantage of having 
an associated supermarket, it appears likely that Woolworths will retain the 
majority of the customers who would have otherwise patronised the Porter’s 
Liquor business, although it may be expected that some will switch to other 
retailers, particularly if seeking convenience and faster checkout service. 
49. 
Post-acquisition there will remain within Jindabyne the Lake Jindabyne Hotel 
Motel walk-in bottle shop (Local Liquor brand) and the Banjo Patterson Inn 
Hotel walk-in bottle shop (Bottlemart Express brand).  Within 5km of Jindabyne, 
on the road from Cooma, is also the East Jindabyne Liquor Supplies (trading as 
‘The Bottlo’).  There are also three other liquor licences within Jindabyne not 
associated with bottle shops, although these are unlikely to impose a constraint 
unless they expand to offer bottle shops.   
50. 
Within Cooma there are at least two non-Woolworths owned bottle shops, 
Liquorland (Coles) and the Cooma Hotel (Local Liquor brand) and five hotels 
with retail liquor licences.

Barriers to entry 
52. 
In previous matters, the ACCC has formed the view that state liquor licensing 
laws are the most significant barrier to entry.  In NSW, new prospective licensees 
must apply to the Licensing Court for approval, including a detailed social impact 
statement in their application.  This may be a prolonged process.  Interim 
approval may be granted prior to the completion of the premises, reducing the 
delay caused by the application process.  Once granted, licenses in rural areas 
may be relocated to new premises within a 5km radius; in this situation the 
application process is significantly less onerous than for a new licence.  Licences 
may also be relocated outside the 5km radius but the approval process is more 
complicated. 
53. 
In order to help independent liquor retailers overcome the barriers to entry, IGA 
has a program to help prospective independent licensees deal with zoning and 
regulatory compliance issues.   
Conclusion on market two 
54. 
It appears unlikely that the acquisition of the Festival IGA Liquor and the 
Porter’s Liquor licence will lead to a substantial lessening of competition.  There 
is no increase in the concentration of ownership of liquor licences within 
Jindabyne compared with the present situation, and it is not clear whether 
Aymblack’s two liquor licences would be held by separate owners in the absence 
of the proposed transaction.  In the absence of strong and credible evidence that 
the counterfactual involves the sale of the licence to separate owners, it is 
difficult to establish any potential substantial lessening of competition. Further, a 
number of competing liquor licences will remain in Jindabyne and Cooma.

57. 
Metcash is the supplier of the majority of the products sold by independent 
supermarkets and independent supermarket chains.  Metcash, including its 
subsidiaries and associates, is the most significant wholesale supplier other than 
Coles and Woolworths to these markets.    
58. 
The loss of sales due to the acquisition may have an amplified effect upon 
Metcash due to high fixed costs associated with grocery wholesaling.  However, 
it appears that the loss of sales as a result of the acquisition will be small relative 
to Metcash’s total sales, as reported in its June 2007 annual report.   
59. 
Therefore, in relation to Markets 3 and 4, it appears unlikely that the transaction 
will significantly decrease the market share or buying power of Metcash in either 
market or significantly increase Woolworths' market share or buying power in 
either market. 
Conclusion on markets three and four 
60. 
It appears unlikely that the effect of the acquisition at the wholesale level will 
substantially lessen competition. 
Other suppliers 
61. 
The businesses stock a number of products from local producers including local 
wines and cheeses.  The businesses are also supplied with transport services 
locally.  A number of other customers and potential customers will remain for 
these businesses and it appears unlikely that the acquisition, which may remove 
one potential customer for these suppliers if Woolworths is not considered a 
potential customer for these local producers, will affect the relevant markets of 
these suppliers in their entirety.   
Conclusion 
62. 
On the basis of the above, the ACCC formed the view that the proposed 
acquisition of Jindabyne Festival IGA Supermarket, Jindabyne Festival IGA 
Liquor and the licence only of Porter's Liquor by Woolworths Limited would not 
be likely to result in a substantial lessening of competition in the relevant markets 
in contravention of section 50 of the Act.
