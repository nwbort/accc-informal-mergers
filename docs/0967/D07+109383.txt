Statement of Issues — A&R Whitcoulls Group 
Holdings Pty Limited (Angus & Robertson) - proposed 
acquisition of Borders Australia Pty Limited 
1. 
Outlined below is the Statement of Issues released by the Australian Competition 
and Consumer Commission (ACCC) in relation to the proposed acquisition of 
Borders Australia Pty Limited (Borders) by A&R Whitcoulls Group Holdings Pty 
Limited (Angus & Robertson) (proposed acquisition).  
2. 
A Statement of Issues published by the ACCC is not a final decision about a 
proposed acquisition, but provides the ACCC’s preliminary views, drawing 
attention to particular issues of varying degrees of competition concern, as well 
as identifying the lines of further inquiry that the ACCC wishes to undertake. 
3. 
In line with the ACCC’s Merger Review Process Guidelines (available on the 
ACCC’s website at www.accc.gov.au) the ACCC has established a secondary 
timeline for further consideration of the issues. The ACCC anticipates completing 
further market inquiries by Wednesday, 5 December 2007 and anticipates making 
a final decision on Wednesday, 19 December 2007. However, the anticipated 
timeline can change in line with the Merger Review Process Guidelines. To keep 
abreast of possible changes in relation to timing and to find relevant documents, 
market participants should visit the Mergers Register on the ACCC's website at 
www.accc.gov.au/mergersregister. 
4. 
A Statement of Issues provides an opportunity for all interested parties (including 
customers, competitors, shareholders and other stakeholders) to ascertain and 
consider the primary issues identified by the ACCC. It is also intended to provide 
the merger parties and other interested parties with the basis for making further 
submissions. 
Background 
5. 
On 8 October 2007, the ACCC commenced its informal review of the proposed 
acquisition in response to Angus & Robertson’s request for informal review.

The parties 
A&R Whitcoulls Group Holdings Pty Limited (Angus & Robertson) 
6. 
Angus & Robertson operates 182 book stores across Australia, including 119 
company-owned stores and 63 franchise stores. Angus & Robertson is owned 
jointly by Pacific Equity Partners (a private equity firm) and Angus & 
Robertson’s current management. 
7. 
Angus & Robertson’s primary business is the retail sale of fiction and non-fiction 
books. Angus & Robertson also retails a small amount of stationery, DVDs and 
videos, newspapers and magazines. It also has an on-line book store. 
Borders Australia Pty Limited (Borders) 
8. 
Borders is owned by its US parent company, Borders Group, Inc. Borders entered 
Australia in 1998. There are currently 22 Borders stores across Australia.  
9. 
Borders’ primary business is the retail sale of fiction and non-fiction books. 
Borders also retails DVDs and videos, CDs, magazines and stationery. Borders 
stores typically also contain a café.  
The transaction 
10. 
On 23 March 2007, the US parent company of Borders, Borders Group, Inc., 
announced the planned sale of Borders’ Australian arm via a competitive sale 
process. 
11. 
Angus & Robertson has submitted a bid to acquire Borders’ entire business in 
Australia. 
Glossary of terms used 
12. 
Back-list: around twelve months after release, a book is placed on the back-list if 
it remains in print.  Back-list books are often not as readily available as front-list 
books 
13. 
Department stores: Myer, David Jones. The department stores stock a limited 
range of key titles, but are not as price competitive as DDS chains, other than on 
their top 10 titles.  
14. 
Discount department stores / chains (DDS): Big W, Kmart, Target. DDS chains 
focus on marketing a limited range of low priced, popular books (key titles) and 
do not rely on range or service as their competitive strengths. This differentiates 
them from the specialist book chains. New release titles comprise a significant 
proportion of book sales by DDS chains. 
15. 
Front-list: newly released books.  
16. 
Key titles: popular titles with broad appeal. Tend to be 'best sellers' or new 
releases by well known authors. Key titles are regarded as ‘must-have’ titles by 
book retailers.

17. 
Range (or non-key) titles: titles that are not, or are unlikely to be, best-sellers. 
Range titles include books by less well known authors and most backlist titles. 
Specialist book stores generally offer a more comprehensive range of range titles 
than department and discount department stores.  
Market inquiries 
18. 
On 12 October 2007, the ACCC commenced market inquiries regarding the 
proposed acquisition. A range of interested parties provided responses, including 
other book retailers, publishers and industry associations.  
19. 
The ACCC is now calling for certain further information from interested parties, 
as set out below. 
Market definition 
20. 
The ACCC’s inquiries have led it to examine the transaction in the context of: 
(i) the national markets for the acquisition of books by retailers (i.e. the 
wholesale market), and  
(ii) a series of local markets for the retail sale of books to consumers.  
21. 
Consumer preferences and travel patterns indicate that competition in the retail 
market takes place at a local level. However, the nature of the merger parties 
(both operate nationally, with many pricing, promotional, and range decisions 
made on a national basis) means that some of the possible adverse impacts on 
competition would occur across the merged entity's entire store network rather 
than being restricted to specific local markets. 
Statement of issues 
22. 
For the purposes of this Statement of Issues, the issues are divided into three 
categories: ‘issues likely to raise concerns’, 'issues that may raise concerns' and 
'issues unlikely to raise concerns'. 
Issues likely to raise concerns 
Potential for a reduction in store-wide discounts or customer loyalty programs 
23. 
While new release ‘frontlist’ books are often sold at a discount to RRP, the 
ACCC’s inquiries indicate that, with the exception of remainders, ‘backlist’ or 
‘range’ books are generally not sold at a discount to RRP on an everyday basis.  
24. 
Instead, ACCC inquiries indicate that the national book retailers tend to compete 
on price across their range via store-wide discount offers, loyalty programs and 
member newsletters.  
25. 
Borders’ discount and loyalty strategies are particularly innovative. Customers 
can subscribe to a weekly email newsletter (‘Borders shortlist’) which contains 
two discount coupons across product categories. Borders also has a ‘3 for 2’

promotional offer whereby three books (from a limited range) can be purchased 
for the price of two.   
26. 
Angus & Robertson, Borders and Dymocks are the only three national book 
chains (i.e. nationwide chains which stock a wide range of book titles, including 
both ‘key titles’ and ‘range titles’, complemented by expert customer service). 
The ACCC notes that the national chain bookstores are each other’s closest 
competitive constraint in terms of these store-wide discount and loyalty offers.  
27. 
The ACCC’s inquiries indicate that, with a few exceptions, smaller bookstore 
chains and independent book retailers tend to focus on service and/or specialty 
titles rather than price. Very few independent retailers offer loyalty programs, and 
they do not commonly offer store-wide discounting.  
28. 
Department and discount department stores do not offer loyalty rewards (other 
than the generic Fly Buys scheme). Market inquiries indicated these stores offer 
much smaller ranges than specialty bookstores, focusing mainly on key titles and 
new releases. Therefore, although discount department stores do, at times, offer 
store-wide discounts and special promotions, their limited book range means that 
customers cannot use these offers to secure discounts on range titles.  
29. 
Therefore the loyalty programs and discount offers by the major chains are the 
major avenue for consumers to purchase 'range' titles at a discounted price. The 
ACCC is concerned that the removal of the competitive tension between Borders 
and Angus & Robertson may reduce the size and/or prevalence of these discounts 
and promotions, leading to higher overall prices paid by consumers. 
30. 
The ACCC invites further information and comments about whether a combined 
Angus & Robertson/ Borders would have the ability and incentive to reduce 
discounting and promotional offers post-acquisition. In particular, the ACCC is 
seeking further comment on: 
A. Are loyalty programs / member newsletters important as a form of 
competition amongst book retailers?  
 Do you have any consumer research, statistics, or relevant 
examples in relation to the above? 
B. How do Angus & Robertson, Borders and Dymocks currently compete 
in this regard?  
 Please provide details of their loyalty programs, member 
newsletters, catalogue activity and store wide discounts.  
C. Do other retailers offer loyalty programs or similar promotions?  If yes, 
please provide details.   
D. Do you consider that the merged firm will have an incentive to change 
the nature of the Angus & Robertson or Borders loyalty programs? Why 
or why not?  
E. In what way, if any, do you consider that consumers would be adversely 
affected by any move to standardise the loyalty programs across the two 
chains?

Increased prices on range titles  
31. 
The proposed acquisition will reduce the number of separately owned national 
book chains from three to two. 
32. 
Although discount department stores offer very competitive prices on the books 
they sell, as noted above they tend to offer much smaller ranges compared to 
specialty bookstores, focusing mainly on key titles and new releases. Discount 
department stores would therefore generally not constrain the merged entity’s 
pricing outside ‘best sellers’.  
33. 
Market inquiries have also suggested that consumers are less price sensitive (and 
less price aware) with range titles than they are with key titles. Range titles are 
generally not advertised, making it difficult for consumers to compare prices 
across book stores.  
34. 
Market inquiries indicated that Angus & Robertson currently sells some books, 
particularly range titles, above recommended retail price. The majority of books 
in Australia do not have the recommended retail price printed on them. 
35. 
The ACCC is concerned that, post-acquisition, the removal of one of the key 
competitors on range titles will give Angus & Robertson the ability and incentive 
to increase the national prices of range titles above recommended retail price 
across the merged entity.  
36. 
The ACCC invites further information and comments about whether a combined 
Angus & Robertson/ Borders is likely to be able to profitably increase prices for 
range titles by a small but significant amount without losing significant sales 
share to competitors.  
In particular, the ACCC is seeking further comment on: 
A. Current level of price competition 
 Is there a price leader with respect to range titles? 
 How would you describe the level of price competition in 
Australia for these titles? 
 If you are a retailer, how do you determine prices for these titles? 
B. Possible effect of the acquisition on prices 
 Do you consider the acquisition will place the merged entity in a 
position where it can increase prices on range titles? 
 Will other retailers, including Dymocks, Collins, QBD and 
independent stores, constrain the merged entity from increasing 
prices? Why or why not? 
 Do you think internet sellers (in particular Amazon) will prevent 
price increases by the merged entity? Why or why not?

Issues that may raise concerns 
Increased negotiating power in the acquisition of books 
37. 
Market inquiries indicated that the merged entity will, on average, constitute over 
25% of a typical Australian publisher’s or distributor’s sales. This is about twice 
the size of the next largest acquirer of books, Dymocks. Many publishers 
considered that a retailer of this size would have the ability to “make or break” a 
title, depending on whether the merged entity stocks it or not.  
38. 
Market inquiries indicated that the acquisition would improve Angus & 
Robertson’s negotiating power with its suppliers.  
39. 
Publishers submitted that, apart from a few blockbuster titles, book sales are 
heavily dependent on in-store purchasing decisions by consumers. Therefore, the 
number of stores choosing to sell a particular title, as well as the in-store 
placement and promotion of that title, will generally be an important determinant 
of its success.   
40. 
Publishers also indicated that, because the acquisition will significantly increase 
Angus & Robertson’s share of retail sales, post-acquisition it will be more costly 
for them to ‘walk away’ from a deal with Angus & Robertson. They therefore 
considered the acquisition would allow Angus & Robertson to decrease the price 
it pays for books or demand other improvements to terms of trade such as 
increased promotional support.  
41. 
The ACCC seeks further information about how any such outcome may impact 
on the decisions of Australian publishers, particularly decisions about the number 
and nature of new titles published, and any impact on their long term viability. 
42. 
The ACCC will also investigate concerns raised by other retailers that Angus & 
Robertson’s increased negotiating power will allow it to demand exclusive access 
to certain new titles or pre-release ‘windows’ for some new titles.  
43. 
The ACCC invites information and comments from publishers, retailers and other 
interested parties regarding these issues, including: 
A. Information about consumer buying behaviour including influences on 
consumer purchasing and title selection. In particular, the ACCC is 
seeking more information on the extent to which consumers seek out 
particular titles (based on publicity, word of mouth, book reviews etc.) 
as opposed to making their purchasing decision in-store. Information 
that would be particularly relevant includes:  
 consumer research surveys prepared for publishers or retailers; 
 information/data regarding market penetration of particular titles 
— for example, estimates of the proportion of new titles that 
achieve significant market penetration (stocked by all retailers 
including independents, discount department stores, i.e., Big W, 
Kmart and Target, and the major book store chains); and

The loss of innovation and non-price competition in the retail of books 
44. 
The ACCC’s market inquiries indicated that Borders is an innovative competitor 
in Australian book retailing, including in relation to non-price factors such as its 
range of books, store layout, and extended opening hours.  
45. 
Borders’ consumer offering is unique in Australia. Being large format stores, 
Borders offers the most comprehensive range of books in Australia and covers all 
genres. Borders stores encourage lengthy shopping trips and browsing, and are 
often located in or near the entertainment precinct of shopping centres, with

extended trading hours. The in-store cafes are a key component of the Borders 
strategy, adding to the unique consumer experience.  
46. 
The proposed acquisition is likely to remove Borders as a separately owned 
vigorous and effective competitor to Angus & Robertson, and this may lead to 
reduced innovation in book retailing. 
47. 
The ACCC invites information and comments regarding these issues, including: 
A. What impact has Borders’ entry had on the Australian book retailing 
industry? 
 Are consumers able to purchase a greater variety of titles? If yes,

 Have you seen evidence of price wars and/or decreased prices?  
 Please give specific examples of Borders’ innovative approach to

B. Do you consider Borders is a ‘leader’? In what ways?  
C. Do you consider that the level of innovation by Borders would be 
reduced if it were acquired by Angus & Robertson? Why or why not? 
D. Would you describe Borders as successful in Australia? Why or why 
not?  
E. How have competitors responded to Borders’ entry? (Note that this

G. Could the discount department stores (Big W, Kmart and Target) or the

Removal of Borders as a significant competitor in local markets 
48. 
Angus & Robertson and Borders operate stores in the same shopping centre/strip 
or central business district (CBD) area in 17 local markets in Australia.   
49. 
The ACCC’s market inquiries indicated that Angus & Robertson and Borders 
stores compete particularly strongly where they are located near each other. 
Market inquiries also indicated that other competitors in those local areas do not 
competitively constrain Angus & Robertson as effectively as Borders does. 
50. 
The proposed acquisition will remove the incentive for competition between 
Angus & Robertson and Borders in the 17 local markets where both have stores. 
The ACCC has concerns that unless they are strongly competitively constrained 
by other retailers, this may lead to reduced discounting and promotional activity 
(and higher overall prices), as well as reduced competition on range, service and 
other non-price factors. 
51. 
The ACCC also has concerns that the proposed acquisition will reduce potential 
competition in local markets where Borders would have entered and competed 
vigorously with Angus & Robertson (or vice versa) if the acquisition did not 
proceed. 
52. 
The ACCC invites information and comments, particularly from retailers, 
regarding these issues, including: 
A. Do book sellers mainly compete (in their prices, discounts, range or 
other factors) with other retailers in the same shopping centre, or in a 
wider area? 
B. What was the effect on other local book retailers when Borders opened a 
store nearby?  
 Please give specific examples and any supporting information, if 
available (e.g. the impact on sales or profits, including if there 
was no impact; the respective locations of Borders and the other 
retailer).  
C. How did other book retailers, including Angus & Robertson, respond to 
Borders opening a store in their local area?  
 For example, did book sellers reduce their prices, increase 
discounting or promotions, increase their range, improve their 
store layout or fittings, exit the market, or was there no change at 
all? Please give specific examples. 
D. Please describe the process for opening a book store in a local area, 
either as an independent store or as part of a chain.  
 Please include details of the costs (e.g. rent, stock, advertising, 
IT and supply systems, store fit-out), planning and time 
involved, as well as any difficulties that may be encountered. 
 If the store were unsuccessful and closed down, which costs 
would be sunk and which could be recovered?

Issues that are unlikely to raise concerns  
53. 
Angus & Robertson and Borders both also retail a selection of DVDs, CDs, 
magazines and stationery.  
54. 
However, market inquiries indicated that Angus & Robertson and Borders each 
have relatively small sales shares in these products and a significant number of 
competitors will remain post-acquisition.  
55. 
The ACCC considers that the proposed acquisition is unlikely to raise concerns in 
relation to these products.
