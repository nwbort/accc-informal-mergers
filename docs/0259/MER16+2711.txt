Dear Interested Party 
Re: Request for submissions: Consortium comprising Brookfield, Qube & 
others – proposed acquisition of Asciano Limited 
The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) is seeking your views on 
the proposed acquisition of the shares and assets of Asciano Limited (Asciano) by various 
members of a consortium comprising Brookfield Infrastructure Partners L.P. (and certain of 
its affiliates) (Brookfield), Qube Holdings Limited (Qube), Global Infrastructure 
Management, LLC (on behalf of itself and its managed funds and clients) (GIP), Canada 
Pension Plan Investment Board (CPPIB), CIC Capital Corporation (CIC Capital), British 
Columbia Investment Management Corporation (bcIMC), GIC Private Limited (GIC) and 
Qatar Investment Authority (QIA) (collectively, the Consortium). 
The ACCC was reviewing two separate proposed acquisitions of Asciano, each of which 
involved some of the consortium members listed above. However, on 15 March 2016 
Asciano announced that it had entered into new agreements with the Consortium. 
Following this announcement, the ACCC ceased its consideration of the two earlier 
proposed acquisitions. 
There are three components to the proposed acquisition: 
 
GIP, CPPIB, CIC Capital, GIC and bcIMC propose to acquire Asciano’s Pacific 
National rail business through a bid vehicle (the rail acquisition) 
 
Qube, Brookfield, GIC, bcIMC and QIA propose to acquire Asciano’s Patrick 
container terminal division (not including Asciano’s 50 per cent interest in ACFS 
Port Logistics Pty Ltd (the ACFS joint venture) or Asciano’s 50 per cent interest in 
Australian Amalgamated Terminals (AAT)) (the ports acquisition) 
 
Brookfield, GIC, bcIMC and QIA propose to acquire Asciano’s Bulk & Automotive 
Port Services (BAPS) division, Asciano’s 50 per cent interest in the ACFS joint 
venture and Asciano’s 50 per cent interest in AAT (the BAPS acquisition). 
Qube will not acquire Asciano’s 50 per cent interest in AAT as part of this transaction, but 
has indicated that it will acquire this interest at a later date1 or nominate a third party buyer.

Information about the proposed acquirers 
a. Qube  
Qube is listed on the Australian Securities Exchange and provides import and export 
logistics services in Australia. Qube currently divides its operations into three 
divisions, which are described below.  
i. 
Qube Ports and Bulk 
Qube Ports and Bulk is a major provider of wharf-side port logistics and port 
management services in Australia. Qube Ports and Bulk offers logistics services, 
management and expertise extending from port and facility development, vessel 
management, warehousing and distribution, stevedoring services and cargo handling 
for automotive, general cargo and dry bulk commodities for both import and export 
supply chains. 
Qube Ports and Bulk operations consist of on-wharf and port precinct facilities in all 
Australian capital city ports and both dry bulk materials and general cargo facilities in 
a further 24 regional port locations. Qube has a range of interests in cargo storage, 
shed operations, materials handling, heavy-haul transport, warehousing and 
distribution operations across a broad mix of industries and critical logistic chains. 
ii. 
Qube Logistics  
Qube Logistics operates services covering road and rail transport, warehousing and 
distribution, container parks and related services, and intermodal logistics hubs 
including rail terminals and international freight forwarding.  
iii. Qube Strategic Assets  
Qube Strategic Assets manages the development of the Moorebank Intermodal Terminal 
and warehouse facilities, as well as the Minto property that is currently under lease to 
Prixcar (a provider of automotive transport, pre-delivery inspection, storage and related 
services).  
iv. AAT 
Qube owns 50 per cent of AAT, with Asciano holding the remaining 50 per cent. AAT 
operates multi-user port terminals with facilities for importing and exporting motor vehicles 
and general cargo. AAT operates automotive terminals at Port Adelaide, Port of Melbourne,

Port Kembla and Port of Brisbane. AAT is not a stevedore, but provides facilities and 
access to stevedores holding licences for AAT terminals. Qube and Asciano provide 
stevedoring services at all of AAT’s terminals. 
v. 
Prixcar 
Qube has an indirect interest in Prixcar Services Pty Limited, a supplier of pre-delivery 
inspection motor vehicle services, which operates at each AAT terminal. Qube has a 50 per 
cent shareholding in K Line Auto Logistics Pty Ltd, which in turn has a 50 per cent interest 
in Prixcar. Toll PDI Investments Pty Ltd holds the remaining 50 per cent of Prixcar. 
b. Brookfield 
Brookfield is a global asset manager with interests in property, renewable power and 
infrastructure assets. Brookfield has approximately USD 200 billion of assets under 
management. Brookfield’s principal Australian businesses are the Dalrymple Bay 
Coal Terminal (DBCT) and Brookfield Rail.  
i. 
Dalrymple Bay Coal Terminal 
The DBCT business comprises a port facility that exports coal mined in the central 
Bowen Basin region of Queensland.  
 
DBCT is owned by the Queensland State Government and is leased to DBCT 
Management (a related entity of Brookfield) through a 50 year lease signed in 2001, 
with a further 49 year option. 
 
DBCT is declared for third party access under the Queensland Competition Authority 
Act 1997 (Qld) with terms and conditions of access regulated by a Queensland 
Competition Authority approved access undertaking.  
 
Further details about the relevant access arrangements at DBCT and copies of the 
relevant access documentation are available at: 
http://www.dbctm.com.au/aboutdbct/documents.aspx.  
ii. 
Brookfield Rail 
Brookfield Rail is a below rail access provider which operates over 5,100 kilometres 
of track and related infrastructure in the southwest region of Western Australia under 
a lease from the Western Australian Government, with approximately 35 years 
remaining.  
 
The Brookfield Rail network is subject to the Railways Act 1998 (WA) and the 
Railways (Access) Code 2000 and is regulated by the Economic Regulatory Authority 
of Western Australia. This regime provides a framework for access to Western 
Australia’s railway network, including Brookfield Rail’s network. 
 
Further information about the access regime is available at: 
https://www.erawa.com.au/rail/rail-access.
